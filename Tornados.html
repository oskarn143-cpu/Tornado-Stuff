Tornados

All You Need To Know About Tornados!

What is a Tornado?

A tornado is a violently rotating column of air that extends from a thunderstorm to the ground. It is one of nature's most powerful and destructive weather phenomena. Tornados can cause extensive damage, with winds reaching speeds of up to 300 mph (480 km/h).

How Tornados Form

Tornados form when warm, moist air meets cool, dry air in an unstable atmosphere. This creates thunderstorms with strong updrafts. When wind shear causes the updraft to rotate, a mesocyclone forms. If conditions are right, a funnel cloud descends from the storm, touching the ground and becoming a tornado.

  • Ingredients for Tornado Formation: Warm, moist air; cool, dry air; wind shear; and instability.
  • Peak Season: Spring and early summer in the U.S., but they can occur year-round.

Types of Tornados

Tornados are classified by their intensity using the Enhanced Fujita Scale (EF Scale), ranging from EF0 (weak) to EF5 (violent).

  • EF0-EF1: Light damage, winds 65-110 mph.
  • EF2-EF3: Considerable damage, winds 111-165 mph.
  • EF4-EF5: Devastating damage, winds 166-200+ mph.

Other types include waterspouts (over water), landspouts, and gustnadoes.

Tornado Safety Tips

If a tornado warning is issued, take shelter immediately. Go to a basement or interior room on the lowest floor. Avoid windows and cover yourself with blankets or helmets.

  • Have an emergency kit with water, food, flashlight, and first aid.
  • Listen to weather radios or apps for updates.
  • If outdoors, lie flat in a ditch and cover your head.
  • Do not try to outrun a tornado in a vehicle.

Famous Tornados

Some notable tornados include the Tri-State Tornado of 1925 (deadliest in U.S. history, 695 deaths) and the Joplin Tornado of 2011 (EF5, 158 deaths).

Myths and Facts

  • Myth: Tornados only occur in Tornado Alley. Fact: They can happen anywhere with the right conditions.
  • Myth: Opening windows equalizes pressure. Fact: This is dangerous and unnecessary.
  • Myth: Highways are safe. Fact: Vehicles can be picked up by strong winds.
